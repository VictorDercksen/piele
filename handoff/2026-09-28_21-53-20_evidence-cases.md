# Evidence cases: members review submitted evidence

## Request

Submitting duty evidence opens a case. Members accept it or veto it with a reason, and the
captain reviews the veto. A majority of accepts accepts the evidence. With no majority and no
veto within 24 hours it is accepted automatically. Product decisions made along the way:

- First event wins: a majority of accepts, a veto, or the 24-hour timer.
- When the captain is involved (the duty is theirs, or they cast the veto), a stand-in reviewer
  rules. The captain nominates the stand-in for the league.
- The captain keeps the direct accept/reject override, which closes the case.
- Ballots are anonymous. No response reveals who voted or vetoed.
- Silence counts as nothing; the timer auto-accepts.
- No eligible voters: accepted at once.
- Only claimed members vote.

## Rules as built

- The electorate is fixed when a case opens: claimed, active members, excluding the duty's
  member and the submitter. A name claimed later does not join an open case.
- Accept can change to veto while voting is open. A veto is final and moves the case to
  review.
- Reviewer: the captain; the stand-in when the captain is the subject or cast the veto; a
  global admin without membership always. A stand-in is barred in the same way. With nobody
  eligible the case stays in review.
- An upheld veto rejects the evidence; the duty reopens for a new submission. A dismissed veto
  reopens voting on the original timer, and accepts at once if the window has closed or a
  majority is already in.
- Accepted evidence completes the duty from the same effective time as before, so the window
  costs no marks.
- There is no scheduler. `cases.settle_due` accepts due cases once per request, inside the
  request transaction, before any duty lock. After locking, the deadline is checked again.
- A released name leaves its live cases (uncast rows removed, majority re-checked). A cast
  ballot stays counted but is visible and editable only by the account that cast it.

## Unresolved

- Under the stand-in rule, a stand-in who can review a veto on a duty that isn't the captain's
  can infer that the captain vetoed. The rule itself causes this.
- Case notifications ride on the feed, with no per-recipient targeting; the client filters
  them using the case fields.
- The web and the API must deploy together. An older API has no `/evidence/cases`, and the
  league load would fail.
